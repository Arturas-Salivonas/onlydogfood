1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

Algorithm Version: 3.1.0
Last Updated: January 19, 2026
Total Possible Score: 100 points

PURPOSE:
This algorithm provides an objective, research-based scoring system for dog
food products based on ingredient quality, nutritional value, and price
competitiveness.

CORE PRINCIPLES:
- Prioritize named, whole meat sources over generic proteins
- Reward protein diversity (multiple animal sources)
- Penalize grain-heavy formulas (high glycemic index)
- Reward fresh/raw and dehydrated meats (transparency & quality)
- Penalize artificial additives and controversial ingredients
- Account for price-to-quality ratio

3. INGREDIENT QUALITY SECTION (45 POINTS)
================================================================================

SUBSECTION BREAKDOWN:

A) Effective Meat Content               : 15 points maximum
B) Protein Source Diversity             : 5 points maximum
C) Low-Value Fillers & Carbohydrates    : 10 points maximum
D) Artificial Additives & Preservatives : 10 points maximum
E) Named Animal Sources                 : 5 points maximum
F) Ingredient-Level Bonus/Penalty       : ±7 points (capped)

Additional Quality Adjustments:
- Ingredient Splitting Penalty          : -5 points
- Filler Stuffing Penalty               : -4 points
- Excessive Ingredients Penalty         : -3 points


---[ A) EFFECTIVE MEAT CONTENT (15 POINTS) ]---

SCORING METHODOLOGY:
Uses effective_meat_percent (moisture-adjusted) or meat_content_percent

Meat Content %    | Base Points | Description
------------------|-------------|------------------------------------------
≥ 50%             | 15.0        | Excellent - premium meat content
40-49%            | 13.0-14.9   | Very Good - substantial meat
30-39%            | 10.0-12.9   | Good - adequate meat content
20-29%            | 6.0-9.9     | Fair - minimal acceptable
< 20%             | 0.0-5.9     | Poor - insufficient meat


FRESH/RAW MEAT BONUS: +1 point
Awarded when ingredient list contains:
- "Fresh [Animal]" (e.g., Fresh Chicken, Fresh Beef, Fresh Salmon)
- "Raw [Animal]" (e.g., Raw Turkey, Raw Whole Herring)
- "Deboned [Animal]" (e.g., Deboned Chicken, Deboned Lamb)

WHY: Indicates manufacturer transparency and commitment to whole food
ingredients rather than generic terms


CONCENTRATED PROTEIN BONUS: +1 point
Awarded when ingredient list contains:
- "Dehydrated [Animal]" (e.g., Dehydrated Chicken, Dehydrated Turkey)
- "Dried [Animal]" (e.g., Dried Salmon, Dried Beef)

WHY: Dehydrated meats contain 300% more protein than fresh meats (water
removed), providing superior protein density

NOTE: Cannot receive both bonuses - whichever applies first


AMBIGUOUS NOTATION PENALTY: -2 points
Penalizes confusing ingredient names such as:
- "Chicken (Chicken Meal)" - unclear which one it is
- "Liver (Heart)" - two ingredients or one?
- "Fish (Salmon)" - why parentheses?

WHY: Red flag for manufacturer attempting to manipulate ingredient order


---[ B) PROTEIN SOURCE DIVERSITY (5 POINTS) ]---

SCORING BASED ON VARIETY:

Diversity Level    | Requirements                | Points | Description
-------------------|----------------------------|--------|------------------
Exceptional        | 3+ types, 6+ sources       | 5      | Orijen-level
Excellent          | 3+ types, 4-5 sources      | 4      | Very diverse
Good               | 2 types, 3+ sources        | 3      | Good variety
Moderate           | 2 types OR 2 sources       | 2      | Some variety
Single-Source      | 1 source only              | 0      | No diversity


PROTEIN TYPE CATEGORIES:

Type          | Examples
--------------|--------------------------------------------------------
Poultry       | Chicken, Turkey, Duck, Goose, Quail, Pheasant
Red Meat      | Beef, Lamb, Venison, Bison, Pork, Wild Boar, Kangaroo
Fish          | Salmon, Herring, Mackerel, Sardine, Trout, Hake, Cod
Eggs          | Whole Eggs, Egg Powder, Dried Egg
Novel         | Insect, Rabbit, Ostrich


EXAMPLE CALCULATION:

Product with: Fresh Chicken, Turkey Meal, Salmon, Herring, Eggs
- Protein Types: 3 (Poultry, Fish, Eggs)
- Total Sources: 5 (chicken, turkey, salmon, herring, egg)
- Result: EXCELLENT = 4 points


---[ C) LOW-VALUE FILLERS & CARBOHYDRATES (10 POINTS) ]---

STARTING SCORE: 10 points (deductions applied)


HIGH-RISK FILLERS: -2 points each
- Corn Gluten Meal
- Wheat Gluten
- Soy Protein Isolate
- By-Product (any type)
- Poultry By-Product
- Meat By-Product

WHY: These artificially inflate protein percentages without providing
quality animal protein


LOW-VALUE CARBS: -2 points each (INCREASED from -1 in v3.0)
- White Rice
- Maize / Corn
- Wheat
- Ground Corn/Maize

WHY: High glycemic index, minimal nutritional value, cheap fillers


BROWN RICE: -1 point each occurrence
- Brown Rice
- Whole Brown Rice
- Wholegrain Brown Rice

WHY: Whole grain but still high glycemic index, acceptable in moderation


GRAIN POSITION PENALTIES (SEVERE):
These stack with the above penalties:

Position                           | Penalty  | Example
----------------------------------|----------|---------------------------
HIGH-GI grain as #1 ingredient    | -8 pts   | "Maize, Chicken Meal..."
Multiple HIGH-GI grains in top 5  | -7 pts   | "Chicken, Rice, Corn..."
HIGH-GI grain in position 2-3     | -5 pts   | "Chicken, Maize, Rice..."
HIGH-GI grain in position 4-5     | -3 pts   | "Chicken, Beef, Rice..."
Brown rice as #1 ingredient       | -4 pts   | "Brown Rice, Chicken..."
Brown rice in position 2-3        | -2 pts   | "Chicken, Brown Rice..."


EXAMPLE CALCULATION:

Ingredients: "Chicken Meal, Brown Rice, Maize, Beet Pulp..."
- Base Score: 10 points
- Low-value carb penalty (maize): -2 points
- Brown rice penalty: -1 point
- Multiple grains in top 5: -7 points
- FINAL SCORE: 0 points (minimum floor applied)


---[ D) ARTIFICIAL ADDITIVES & PRESERVATIVES (10 POINTS) ]---

STARTING SCORE: 10 points (deductions applied)


RED FLAG ADDITIVES: Automatic 0 points for entire subsection
- Ethoxyquin (banned in some countries)
- Propylene Glycol (antifreeze component)

WHY: Linked to serious health concerns, unacceptable in any dog food


ARTIFICIAL COLORS: Automatic 0 points for entire subsection
- Red 40, Yellow 5, Yellow 6, Blue 2
- Artificial Color/Colour
- Caramel Color
- Ferrous Oxide (coloring)

WHY: No nutritional value, potential allergens, purely cosmetic


ARTIFICIAL PRESERVATIVES: Graduated penalties
First preservative: -3 points
Each additional: -2 points
≥3 preservatives: Automatic 0 points

Preservatives in this category:
- BHA (Butylated Hydroxyanisole)
- BHT (Butylated Hydroxytoluene)
- TBHQ (Tertiary Butylhydroquinone)
- Propyl Gallate

WHY: Controversial safety profile, natural alternatives exist


CONTROVERSIAL ADDITIVES: -3 points each
- Carrageenan (digestive issues)
- Guar Gum (can cause bloating)
- Xanthan Gum
- Sodium Selenite (toxic in high doses)
- Menadione (synthetic Vitamin K)

WHY: Legal but linked to digestive problems in sensitive dogs


---[ E) NAMED ANIMAL SOURCES (5 POINTS) ]---

SCORING:

Ingredient Profile                      | Points | Example
----------------------------------------|--------|------------------------
All named sources, no generic           | 5.0    | "Chicken Meal, Salmon"
Mix of named + generic                  | 2.5    | "Chicken, Meat Meal"
Only generic/unnamed sources            | 0.0    | "Meat Meal, Poultry"


NAMED SOURCES (GOOD):
- Chicken, Beef, Lamb, Turkey, Duck, Salmon, Herring, etc.
- Specific animal clearly identified


UNNAMED SOURCES (BAD):
- "Meat Meal" - what animal?
- "Poultry Meal" - chicken? turkey? both?
- "Animal Protein" - completely generic
- "Fish Meal" - what type of fish?
- "Game" - what species?

WHY: Named sources ensure traceability and quality control

OPTIMAL RANGE: 22-32% protein (as-fed basis)

Protein %       | Base Points | Description

20-21%          | 10.0-11.9   | Minimum acceptable
> 40%           | 8.0-10.0    | Too high for average dog
< 20%           | 5.0-8.0     | Insufficient


PLANT PROTEIN PENALTY:
If product contains significant plant protein (pea protein, soy, etc.)
AND has low meat content (< 30%):

Penalty = (Plant Protein % ÷ 10) × -1
Maximum penalty: -3 points

Example: 15% pea protein = -1.5 point penalty

WHY: Plant proteins are incomplete, lack essential amino acids

5. VALUE FOR MONEY SECTION (22 POINTS)
================================================================================

SUBSECTION BREAKDOWN:

A) Price Competitiveness : 15 points maximum
B) Ingredient Value      : 7 points maximum


---[ A) PRICE COMPETITIVENESS (15 POINTS) ]---

Compares product's price per kg against category average

Price vs Category Avg  | Points | Description
-----------------------|--------|----------------------------------
≤ 60% of average       | 15.0   | Exceptional value
61-80% of average      | 13.0   | Excellent value
81-95% of average      | 11.0   | Good value
96-110% of average     | 9.0    | Fair value
111-130% of average    | 7.0    | Slightly expensive
131-150% of average    | 5.0    | Expensive
> 150% of average      | 3.0    | Very expensive


---[ B) INGREDIENT VALUE (7 POINTS) ]---

Quality-adjusted value score
Considers ingredient quality relative to price

High quality + low price    = 7 points
High quality + fair price   = 5-6 points
Medium quality + low price  = 4-5 points
Medium quality + fair price = 3-4 points
Low quality + any price     = 1-2 points

---[ EXAMPLE 1: CHICKEN IN DIFFERENT FORMS ]---

Ingredient                        | Category              | Points | Moisture
----------------------------------|----------------------|--------|----------
Dehydrated Chicken                | Concentrated Meal    | +4     | ~10%
Chicken Meal                      | Concentrated Meal    | +4     | ~10%
Fresh Chicken                     | Premium Fresh Meat   | +3     | ~70%

Chicken (generic)                 | Named Meat           | 0      | varies
Poultry (generic)                 | Unnamed Protein      | -4     | varies
Poultry Meal (generic)            | Unnamed Protein      | -4     | ~10%

EXPLANATION:

Dehydrated/Meal Chicken (+4):
- Water removed through dehydration
- ~300% more protein per gram than fresh
- Concentrated nutrient density
- Named source (chicken specifically identified)
- Gets CONCENTRATED PROTEIN BONUS in calculator

Fresh/Raw Chicken (+3):
- 70% moisture content
- Lower protein density per gram
- High quality, whole food ingredient
- Named source
- Gets FRESH/RAW MEAT BONUS in calculator

Generic Chicken (0):
- Just "chicken" without qualifier
- Unknown processing method
- Named but no transparency bonus

Poultry/Poultry Meal (-4):
- UNNAMED source - could be chicken, turkey, duck, or mix
- Unknown quality, unknown origin
- Red flag for lack of transparency


WHY THE DIFFERENCE?

Fresh Chicken:
- 100g fresh chicken = ~18g protein (70% water)
- Excellent quality but lower density

Dehydrated Chicken:
- 100g dehydrated = ~65g protein (10% water)
- 3.6x more protein per gram!
- Higher score reflects superior nutrient density

Fresh gets +1 bonus for transparency/quality signal
Dehydrated gets +1 bonus for concentration
Both are NAMED sources (good)
Generic "Poultry" is UNNAMED (bad) = -4 penalty

   - Manipulates ingredient order

   - Maize, Corn, White Rice, Wheat as first ingredient
   - Indicates grain-based food, not meat-based

7. Multiple High-GI Grains in Top 5
   - More grains than meat sources
   - Poor quality formula

8. By-Products
   - Meat By-Product, Poultry By-Product
   - Undefined waste products

9. Generic Proteins
   - "Meat Meal", "Poultry Meal", "Animal Protein"
   - Unknown source and quality

10. Added Sugar
    - Sugar, Corn Syrup, Molasses
    - Promotes obesity, no benefit
